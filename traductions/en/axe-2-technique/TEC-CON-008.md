::The sea holds 97% of the planet's water, and you can't drink a glass of it. Desalination means paying in energy for what nature does for free with sun and rain.::

## Why you can't drink seawater

Seawater contains about **35 grams of salt per litre**. Drinking water contains less than 0.5 to 1 gram.

Kidneys can't make urine as salty as the sea. To get rid of the salt in one litre of seawater, they need more than one litre of water. **Drinking seawater dehydrates you faster than drinking nothing.** That's why castaways who drink it die before the others, and what makes desalination essential at sea. See [[TEC-SAN-005]].

## The two big methods

### Heating: distillation

You boil the water, the steam leaves without the salt, you condense it. That's what the sun does above the oceans.

Modern plants don't simply boil: they chain chambers at lower and lower pressure, where water boils at lower and lower temperatures, and recover the heat from each stage to warm the next. These are **multi-stage flash** (MSF) and **multi-effect distillation** (MED). They use a lot of energy, and are found mostly in the Gulf, often paired with power stations whose waste heat they recover.

### Filtering: reverse osmosis

A very thin membrane lets water molecules through and holds back salts. Naturally, water would move towards the saltier side (that's osmosis). **By pushing seawater against the membrane at 55 to 80 bar** (800 to 1,200 psi), you force it through the other way.

This is now the dominant method: **nearly 70% of the world's capacity**. A modern plant uses about **3 to 4 kWh per cubic metre** of fresh water, thanks to recovery devices that reuse the pressure of the outgoing brine.

The process needs:

- **serious pre-treatment**: filtration, sometimes flocculation, so that sand, algae and bacteria don't clog the membranes;
- **post-treatment**: reverse-osmosis water is too pure, aggressive to pipes and poor in minerals. Calcium is added back and the pH corrected.

## The numbers

- The world desalinates **about 95 million cubic metres a day** (late 2010s), in more than 15,000 plants.
- The Middle East and North Africa produce nearly half. Saudi Arabia, the Emirates and Israel depend on it for a large share of their drinking water.
- The largest reverse-osmosis plant in the world, at Sorek in Israel, produces more than 600,000 m³ a day.
- From 100 litres of seawater, a reverse-osmosis plant returns **about 40 to 50 of fresh water**. The rest goes back as **brine**, twice as salty as the sea.

## What doesn't work so well

**Energy.** Even efficient, a plant uses ten times more than pumping fresh water. A power cut stops it, and tap water with it.

**Brine.** Released into the sea, it sinks to the bottom and can smother local life if it isn't well diffused. It also carries pre-treatment chemicals.

**Membranes** foul and are replaced every five to ten years. They are made by a handful of manufacturers.

**The target.** A desalination plant is a single piece of infrastructure feeding an entire city. The Gulf countries protect them like power stations.

## At small scale

- **The manual watermaker** in life rafts: a small hand-pumped reverse-osmosis unit that makes a few litres an hour. It's the one piece of equipment that really changes survival at sea.
- **Boat watermakers**: electric, from 20 to several hundred litres an hour. They live on the boat's batteries and solar panels.
- **The solar still**: a black tank under a sloping pane of glass. It makes around **2 to 5 litres per square metre per day** in full sun. Slow, but with no moving parts.
- **Old sea ice is almost fresh**: as it freezes, water pushes the salt out. Bluish, rounded ice more than a year old can be drunk once melted. Young, grey, opaque ice stays salty.

## Tips you learn at sea

- **Never keep the first water** from a watermaker after it has been stopped: it's salty. Taste it before filling the tank.
- **A membrane that dries out dies.** When stopped, it's kept wet with a storage solution, or run regularly.
- **Near a port or a river mouth**, seawater is polluted with hydrocarbons and discharges: they damage membranes and aren't all held back. Desalinate offshore.
- **Rain is still the best fresh water at sea.** A tarp stretched into a funnel gives more than a still. See [[SURV-PLUIE-001]].
