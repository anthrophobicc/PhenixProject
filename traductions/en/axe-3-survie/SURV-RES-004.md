::A stone feeds no one. A shoe, hardly. But both soups tell something true: one about hunger, the other about what people give each other when they're given the chance.::

## UNDERSTAND

### The tale

A hungry traveller arrives in a village. Nobody will give him anything. He sets a pot of water on the fire, drops in a well-washed stone, and announces he's making a delicious soup. The villagers, curious, come closer. "It would be better with an onion," he says. Someone brings one. Then a carrot, a piece of bacon, salt, potatoes. In the end, the whole village eats a real soup. The stone gave nothing.

The tale exists all over Europe, with a nail, a button or an axe instead of the stone.

### What's true in it

**The stone doesn't feed.** It brings no calories. At most, a limestone pebble releases a few minerals into an acidic broth.

**But the mechanism is real.** Everyone has a little, nobody has enough, and nobody wants to be the first to give. It takes one person to set the pot down and make sharing easy, visible and joyful. Crisis kitchens, soup kitchens and street meals after a disaster work exactly like this. See [[SURV-SOC-001]].

### Shoe soup

The image comes from the cinema: in The Gold Rush (1925), Charlie Chaplin's prospector, starving and snowed into a cabin, boils his shoe and eats it like a roast, with the laces as spaghetti.

Reality came before the film:

- **The Donner Party**, trapped by snow in the Sierra Nevada in 1846, boiled ox hides to get a jelly out of them.
- **During the siege of Leningrad** (1941 to 1944), people boiled belts, bags and leather book bindings, and scraped off wallpaper paste, which was made of flour.
- Sailors, encircled soldiers and polar explorers ate their harnesses and their boots.

### What leather is really worth

Leather is skin, so mostly **collagen**: a protein. Boiled for a long time, raw hide gives a jelly, like the gelatin from a pig's trotter. So there is a little food in it.

**But:**

- collagen is an incomplete protein: it lacks essential amino acids, it isn't enough on its own;
- **most modern leather is chrome-tanned**. Chromium, glues, dyes and synthetic soles have no business in a stomach;
- a shoe today often contains more plastic than leather.

**Verdict**: fresh or dried hide, or vegetable-tanned leather (raw, beige, undyed), boiled for a long time, gives a little protein. A sneaker, nothing at all.

## ACT

### Making a real soup from almost nothing

Soup is the best way to feed many people with little: nothing is lost, all the fat and minerals stay in the water, and something hot lifts morale as much as the body.

1. **Fat first.** A piece of bacon, oil, a rind. It's what runs short most in hard times. See [[TEC-ALI-001]].
2. **Something starchy** for calories: potatoes, stale bread, rice, pasta, flour stirred in, oat flakes.
3. **Anything green and edible**: carrot and radish tops, nettles, dandelions, cabbage leaves. See [[SURV-RES-001]].
4. **Bones**, even already gnawed, cracked and simmered for a long time: marrow, fat, calcium.
5. **Salt**, which makes everything edible.

### Making stone soup for real

In a group, a camp, a street after a disaster: **put the pot in the middle, in plain sight**, and announce a soup for everyone. Ask each person for one single thing, a small one. It works better than asking one person for a lot.

## ADAPT

**You really only have leather.** Pick raw hide or undyed leather. Scrape it, cut it into strips, boil it for hours, throw away the first water. Eat the jelly, not the strips.

**The hunger has lasted a long time.** Start eating again very gently, in small amounts, or the body can collapse: this is refeeding syndrome.

**There are children.** They get served first, and they're the ones to send to ask for an onion: nobody says no.
