::Sorting is the skill, not collecting. Whatever you take by mistake, you carry for nothing.::

## UNDERSTAND

Three questions decide the fate of every object you pick up, and they come in this order.

**Does it still work, or can I fix it with what I know how to do?** A broken object you can't repair isn't a resource, it's weight.

**How much does it weigh compared to what it gives back?** That's the real criterion, and the one most often neglected. A heavy object has to give a lot, all the time, or it will be dropped somewhere along the way.

**Does it replace several things?** One versatile object beats three specialized ones.

### What ages well, what ages badly

**Plain metal almost never dies.** A blade, a file, a wrench, pliers, a hammer clean up and become as good as new. Even heavily rusted, they can be saved as long as the rust hasn't eaten all the way through — how to assess it is in [[TEC-COR-001]].

**Glass and ceramics** are inert and last forever. A jar with an intact seal is worth a lot for [[TEC-CONS-001]].

**Thick natural textiles** last a long time if they've stayed dry. A tarp, a wool blanket, a rope only wear out from damp and rodents.

**Rubber and soft plastics age badly.** They harden, crack and break under ultraviolet light and time. A seal, a hose, an elastic band or an inner tube stored outside is usually dead. A fold that turns white or cracks when you test it is the sign.

**Electronics die through their capacitors and batteries**, not their chips. A device that kept a swollen battery inside for years is often lost to internal corrosion. See [[TEC-ENE-001]].

**Batteries** leak and destroy whatever is around them. The first reflex with any device you find is to open the battery compartment.

### Ranking by real value

In descending order, more or less regardless of the situation:

**Hand tools.** They consume nothing, don't break down, let you make and repair everything else, and trade extremely well — see [[SURV-TROC-001]].

**What holds things.** Jerrycans, buckets, jars, dry bags. Always underestimated, always in short supply. Without containers, you can't store water or food.

**What ties things.** Rope, straps, wire, sewing thread, tape. Light, with countless uses, and essential to everything in [[TEC-MAC-001]].

**What cuts.** Blades, files, saws. The file deserves a special mention: it lets you put an edge back on, and so bring every other cutting tool back to life — the method is in [[TEC-MEC-005]].

**What protects.** A tarp, thick gloves, sturdy shoes, glasses.

**What makes fire.** See [[SURV-FEU-001]].

## ACT

**Assessing an object in under a minute.**

1. **Pick it up and weigh it in your hand.** Too heavy for what it does: put it back, unless you have a specific use in mind.
2. **Test the moving part.** Hinge, thread, mechanism. That's where things die.
3. **Bend what should be flexible.** It cracks or turns white: it's done.
4. **Scrape the corrosion** on a small spot to see whether sound metal is left underneath.
5. **Open the battery compartment** of any device and look for corrosion.
6. **Look for markings**: dimensions, thread, voltage, part number. That's what will let you find a compatible part later.

**Sort before you carry.** Make three piles on the spot: what I take now, what I leave here in a cache to come back for, what I abandon. The second pile is the one people forget to make, and it's often the most worthwhile — marking the spot on a map layer lets you come back to it.

## ADAPT

**You're on foot and far from home.** Weight becomes the only criterion. Rope, blades, folding containers, textiles. Anything heavy stays where it is, marked for a return trip.

**You have a fixed base.** The math flips: what's heavy and durable gains value, because you won't have to carry it. Heavy tools, rigid containers, spare parts.

**You want to trade.** What trades best is small, widely wanted and divisible. Salt, blades, batteries, medicine, thread. The full reasoning is in [[SURV-TROC-001]].

**You're searching a whole building.** The method and the dangers are in [[SURV-REC-001]].

**You don't know whether it'll be useful.** Then it won't be. The temptation to take something because it might come in handy is the surest way to end up loaded down, slow and visible.
